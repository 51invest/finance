Credit Relationships

A credit relationship moves purchasing power across time under contract: the lender gives up funds now, and the borrower promises to repay principal and interest later. Enforceability, information and collateral decide whether the relationship works.

LevelFoundation

Definition

A credit relationship is a contract between a lender (the creditor) and a borrower (the debtor) that moves purchasing power across time: the lender provides funds now, and the borrower promises to repay the principal with interest later. Principal, interest rate, maturity, repayment schedule, collateral and default terms are its basic elements. A coupon bond is a standardised credit relationship: the coupon is the periodic interest paid, par value is repaid at maturity, and default risk is the risk that payments are delayed or do not occur [2].

Intuition

A bakery owner borrows 10,000 yuan from a neighbour to buy a delivery van, promising to repay principal and interest in one year. The neighbour lends because three things can be checked: the owner has stable cash flow, the van can generate income, and on default there is an enforceable claim or collateral. Amounts throughout are in yuan (CNY) and are teaching assumptions.

Formal expression

Under simple interest (a teaching convention):

Interest = Principal × Annual rate × Years

When principal and interest are paid at maturity:

Repayment at maturity = Principal + Interest

Simple interest ignores compounding and fees. Years is measured in years, so a term that is not a whole number of years is converted from months (12 months count as 1 year, 18 months as 1.5 years). Comparisons across loans should use the same annualised basis.

Example and calculation

A hypothetical loan:

Item Amount
Principal 10,000 yuan
Annual rate (simple) 6%
Term 12 months
Payment schedule Principal and interest at maturity
Total interest 600 yuan
Repayment at maturity 10,600 yuan
Collateral value at start 12,000 yuan
Collateral value at end (assumed fall) 7,000 yuan

Calculation: 10,000 × 6% = 600 yuan; 10,000 + 600 = 10,600 yuan. The loan-to-value ratio is outstanding principal divided by collateral value: initially 10,000 ÷ 12,000 ≈ 83.3%; if collateral falls to 7,000 yuan while 10,000 yuan of principal is still outstanding, the ratio rises to 10,000 ÷ 7,000 ≈ 142.9%. The collateral no longer covers the principal, so the lender faces a larger loss.

Conditions and limits

  1. Enforceability. Credit rests on contracts, property rights and courts, stated here as this article's own framing of the premise. When a promise is hard to enforce a lender's response is not limited to one option: it can raise the rate, shrink the line, demand collateral or a third-party guarantee, shorten the term, add acceleration and recourse clauses, or simply decline to lend. This article lists these as defensive moves available in principle; whether a given move is enforceable, and at what cost, depends on the jurisdiction, which the article has not checked jurisdiction by jurisdiction, so no claim of universal availability is made.
  2. Information and incentives. Borrowers know more about their repayment capacity than lenders do, creating adverse selection; after receiving funds they may change how the money is used, creating moral hazard. Screening, disclosure, covenants and collateral are responses.
  3. Repayment capacity comes from cash flow. Collateral is property the lender can seize or sell when the borrower fails to repay [1], but it cannot replace capacity. If operating cash flow is insufficient, collateral can reduce the loss but cannot prevent default.
  4. Collateral values move. A fall in collateral values raises the loan-to-value ratio and lowers collateral coverage, and can trigger margin calls or tighter lending, amplifying the credit cycle.
  5. Credit is not equity. Creditors receive the agreed interest and principal and do not share in a company's growth gains; borrowers keep the residual gain and bear the downside.
  6. Measurement. The Federal Reserve's G.19 release measures most credit extended to individuals, excluding real-estate-secured loans, and separates revolving from nonrevolving credit [3]. An aggregate does not describe one contract's maturity or risk.

Common mistakes

  • Credit is trust. Trust lowers transaction costs, but credit is enforced through contracts and claims. Without enforcement, large long-term financing is hard to sustain.
  • Credit is money. A loan is the borrower's liability while money is an asset of its holder; they are not the same thing.
  • Collateral removes risk. Collateral can fall in value and is costly to sell, and claims are subject to priority rules. A secured loan still carries credit risk.
  • Borrowed money is income. Borrowing adds an asset and a liability at the same time and does not raise net worth; interest is the cost.
  • The quoted rate is the whole cost. Fees, day-count conventions and compounding change the effective cost, so compare loans on a common annualised basis.

Exercises

  1. Concept check. A bank makes a 30-year mortgage of 800,000 yuan at 4.5%, secured by a house. Identify the creditor, debtor, principal and collateral, and explain who bears the loss if house prices fall.
  2. Calculation. A borrower takes 20,000 yuan at 5% simple interest for 18 months, with principal and interest paid at maturity. What are total interest and total repayment?
  3. Conditions. A borrower has stable cash flow but no collateral, and the lender demands collateral. What roles can collateral play, and what are its limits?
Suggested answers
  1. The bank is the creditor, the borrower the debtor, the principal 800,000 yuan, the house the collateral. A price fall first reduces collateral coverage. If the borrower defaults, who bears the shortfall is set by the contract and the law of the place: without recourse the lender absorbs most of it, with recourse the borrower still owes the difference. This article only works out the arithmetic of those two arrangements; it does not claim which one applies in China, in any U.S. state, or elsewhere in a given year - recourse rules, foreclosure procedure and tax treatment all need checking against the contract text and local law.
  2. Taking 18 months as 1.5 years: 20,000 × 5% × 1.5 = 1,500 yuan; repayment is 20,000 + 1,500 = 21,500 yuan.
  3. Collateral can screen borrowers, strengthen the incentive to repay and reduce recovery losses. Its limits include valuation and disposal costs, legal priority rules and falling collateral prices. Collateral does not eliminate credit risk.
